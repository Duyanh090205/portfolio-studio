01

The Problem & Opportunity

Big ice cream chains sell the scoop as an ordinary treat; none of them make the scoop itself the reason a group of friends stops to share a moment together. brief

3 of 3

Category leaders reviewed — Baskin Robbins, Häagen-Dazs, Van Leeuwen — sell flavor and order-now urgency; none sell the group hangout occasion. desk research, unverified

Gen Z / young millennials

CEMMY's target already turns an ice cream run into a mini hangout, chasing the prettiest scoop worth pulling out a phone for before it melts. brief

HCMC, accessible-premium

Ho Chi Minh City scoop shops and delivery, priced between mass chains and ultra-premium pints, expanding to other cities. assumed

The opening isn't a better flavor claim — it's a better reason to leave the house.

02

The Insight & Positioning

"Nobody plans an ice-cream run in advance — they show up where a friend is already sitting, so the invite has to come from the shop, not the calendar." assumed

For Saigon friend groups who turn an ice cream run into a plan, CEMMY is the neon-lit berry parlour that gives an ordinary evening somewhere to happen, because one swirl of strawberry and blueberry under a lit-up star is worth leaving the house for.

Positioning: the ritual, not the recipe, is the product.
