05: The gap

Against CEMMY's audience — Gen Z and young millennials in Ho Chi Minh City who turn an ice cream run into a mini hangout worth photographing — the category above skews toward selling the product itself, not the moment around it (desk research, unverified).

1. Nobody sells the hangoutThe category's hooks and CTAs above are almost all product-first (flavor, texture, order now). None frame the ad around a group of friends deciding where to go tonight — CEMMY can own the "meet-up occasion" instead of the flavor claim.
2. Nobody owns an evening ritual cueThe visual patterns above are daylight studio shots or flat lays. None use a signature lit landmark as the reason to show up at dusk — CEMMY's glowing marquee star is a ritual cue no competitor has.
3. Nobody localizes the street sceneCopy and visuals above read as generic global assets. None lean into an HCMC evening-parlour, bulb-lit, pink-awning aesthetic that feels like a specific place worth pulling out a phone for — that's CEMMY's built-in shareability.

06: Three concepts

The Star's On
Angle: the evening invite
The lit star sign is the daily cue that the parlour is the plan tonight — turns a passive storefront light into a scroll-stopping "we're open, come sit" moment, geo-boosted 6-9pm. Wins because it needs no discount to work: the ritual is the offer.
Built To Split
Angle: the social object
Makes the strawberry-blueberry swirl itself the reason two people show up together — one scoop, two spoons, framed as teamwork before the first bite. Wins because it turns a flavor fact into a shareable social object, something no competitor's product-shot ads do.
Tag Your Star Table
Angle: the UGC mechanic
Every table sits under its own numbered star light; claiming and tagging it is the whole post. Wins because it scales itself through repost and check-ins, building a visible map of stars across the city that a static product ad can't replicate.
